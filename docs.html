How the dock works.

Everything a sailor — or an agent — needs to mint a Stromboat: two lanes, fixed rules, no surprises.

The collection in one breath

Stromboat is a 1,333-token ERC-721 collection on BNB Chain. Four hulls (Dinghy, Junk, Longship, Lantern Raft) sail five seas, and every token's hull and sea were assigned by its number before launch: token IDs 1–667 are the free Common pool, IDs 668–1,333 are the paid rarities. Twenty artworks in total — every hull in every sea — served from this site under /nft/images/, with metadata under /nft/metadata/.

Both lanes open at the same moment and never block each other. When the free pool is gone, the paid lane keeps sailing — and the other way around.

Minting with a wallet

  1. Open the Launch Terminal on the home page and connect a wallet on BNB Chain (chain id 56). The page switches networks for you.
  2. Free: type your Binance Agent ID number into the free lane and claim. Your wallet must own that Agent ID — the contract checks the official registry on-chain.
  3. Paid: pick 1–7 boats and launch. The page reads the live price from the contract and sends exactly price × quantity.
  4. Your boat's art is fixed by its token number. The terminal reveals a fleet artwork as a preview; the exact piece lives in the token's metadata.

Minting with an agent

The Agent Harbour Office on the home page carries the copy-ready prompt and the baw CLI blocks for both lanes. In short:

Free — freeMint(uint256), selector 0x7c928fe9, argument = your Agent ID as a 32-byte word, value 0.

Paid — mint(uint256), selector 0xa0712d68, argument = quantity (1–7) as a 32-byte word, value = currentPrice() × quantity, read live from the contract.

The contract address is published on the Contracts page and wired into the home page the moment it is deployed. Never take an address from a DM or a reply.

Checking a wallet

The Launch Terminal's wallet checker reads the contract directly: whether the free Common is still available to that wallet, and how many of its 7 paid boats remain. The contract also exposes freeMinted(), paidMinted(), totalMinted(), currentPrice() and mintOpen() for anyone — or any agent — to read.
